# Business Insights — Customer Churn

**Audience:** retention, marketing and customer-experience teams.
**Basis:** XGBoost churn model evaluated on 1,409 held-out historical customers (test set),
explained with SHAP. Numbers come from `reports/` (generated by `python main.py`).

> **Read this first.** The model finds *patterns associated with* churn in historical data.
> It does not prove that changing a customer attribute (e.g. their contract) would stop them
> leaving. Every action below is a **hypothesis to test**, not a demonstrated result.

---

## 1. Which customers are most at risk?

The model ranks customers well: among the 10% of test customers it scored highest, **75.2%
actually churned**, versus 26.5% across all customers (≈2.8× the base rate). In the top 20%, 68.8%
churned.

Predicted risk vs observed churn in the test set (`reports/segment_risk_summary.csv`):

| Segment | Customers | Mean predicted risk | Observed churn | Flagged by model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contract: Month-to-month | 773 | 42.8% | 42.6% | 64.0% |
| Contract: One year | 300 | 10.6% | 12.0% | 2.7% |
| Contract: Two year | 336 | 3.6% | 2.7% | 0.0% |
| Tenure 0–6 months | 311 | 51.6% | 55.6% | 74.6% |
| Tenure 49–72 months | 450 | 9.6% | 7.8% | 7.3% |
| Internet: Fiber optic | 613 | 42.1% | 41.1% | 62.3% |
| Payment: Electronic check | 474 | 44.3% | 43.5% | 66.2% |
| No Online Security (has internet) | 701 | 41.8% | 41.1% | 61.2% |
| No Tech Support (has internet) | 702 | 41.2% | 41.2% | 61.0% |
| Senior citizen | 222 | 40.6% | 44.1% | 60.8% |

**Highest-risk profile:** a recent customer (first months of tenure) on a **month-to-month contract**,
with **fiber-optic internet** and **high monthly charges**, **without online security or tech support**,
paying by **electronic check**. The single highest-scored test customer (90.7%) matches it exactly:
tenure 1 month, month-to-month, fiber optic, 95.10 per month, no security/support, electronic check.

**Lowest-risk profile:** long-tenure customers on **two-year contracts**; the lowest-scored test
customer (0.7%) has 72 months of tenure, a two-year contract and no internet service.

## 2. Most important predictive variables (SHAP)

Share of total mean |SHAP| (log-odds), test set (`reports/shap_feature_importance.csv`):

| Rank | Variable | Share | Direction of the effect in the model |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contract | 26.4% | Month-to-month raises risk; one/two-year contracts lower it |
| 2 | tenure | 15.0% | Short tenure raises risk; long tenure lowers it |
| 3 | OnlineSecurity | 9.0% | "No" raises risk |
| 4 | InternetService | 8.5% | Fiber optic raises risk; no internet lowers it |
| 5 | MonthlyCharges | 7.9% | Higher charges raise risk |
| 6 | TechSupport | 6.8% | "No" raises risk |
| 7 | PaymentMethod | 5.7% | Electronic check raises risk |

These seven variables account for ~79% of the model's attributions. `gender`, `Partner`,
`PhoneService` and `DeviceProtection` contribute almost nothing (<0.5% each).

## 3. Segments to prioritise in a retention campaign

Ordered by the combination of risk and size:

1. **New month-to-month customers (tenure ≤ 6 months).** Highest risk (≈52–56%); the model flags
   three in four. *Early-life* experience is the natural focus.
2. **Fiber-optic customers without security/tech-support add-ons.** Large group (~600 of every
   1,400 customers have fiber) with ~41% churn.
3. **Electronic-check payers.** ~44% churn and about a third of the base (most of them are also month-to-month).
4. **Senior citizens.** Smaller group (16% of customers), 44% observed churn.

In practice, the predictions file `reports/customer_churn_predictions.csv` already ranks customers
and lists each one's top SHAP drivers, so the campaign can be tailored per customer (for example,
customers whose top driver is `TechSupport=No` versus `Contract=Month-to-month`).

## 4. Actions a telecom company could study (hypotheses)

| Hypothesis (to be tested) | Linked model signal | How to test |
|---|---|---|
| Incentives to move from month-to-month to 12/24-month contracts reduce churn | Contract is the strongest driver | Randomised offer to a subset of high-risk month-to-month customers vs a control group |
| A structured onboarding programme in the first 6 months reduces early churn | Short tenure is the 2nd driver | Pilot onboarding (welcome call, check-ins) for new customers; compare 6-month churn |
| Bundling/trialling Online Security and Tech Support lowers churn | Lack of these services raises risk | Free trial offered at random to eligible high-risk customers |
| Investigating fiber-optic service quality and price perception | Fiber + high charges raise risk | Survey/NPS analysis and network-quality data for fiber churners |
| Encouraging automatic payment methods reduces churn | Electronic check raises risk | A/B test a small incentive to switch to automatic payment |

Important caveats: customers who *choose* long contracts or add-ons may differ from those who don't
(self-selection). Moving a customer onto a contract will not necessarily give them the low risk
of customers who chose it. Only controlled experiments can measure the real effect.

## 5. Illustrative economic impact (hypothetical assumptions)

**Assumptions (not company data):** each targeted customer costs **30** (contact + incentive);
a targeted customer who would have churned is retained with probability **25%**; a retained
customer is worth **12 months × MonthlyCharges × 50% margin**. Contacting a customer who would have
stayed costs money and saves nothing.

On the 1,409 test customers (`reports/business_impact_scenarios.csv`):

| Strategy | Targeted | Churners reached | Cost | Expected value saved | **Expected net value** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model, selected threshold (0.33) | 503 | 280 | 15,090 | 31,676 | **16,586** |
| Model, F2 threshold (0.19) | 680 | 321 | 20,400 | 35,911 | 15,511 |
| Model, default threshold (0.50) | 297 | 200 | 8,910 | 23,092 | 14,182 |
| Random targeting, same volume (503) | 503 | 124 | 15,090 | 13,122 | −1,968 |
| Target everyone | 1,409 | 374 | 42,270 | 40,822 | −1,448 |

Sensitivity to the retention success rate (net value):

| Strategy | 10% success | 25% success | 40% success |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model, selected threshold (0.33) | −2,420 | 16,586 | 35,592 |
| Model, default threshold (0.50) | 327 | 14,182 | 28,037 |
| Target everyone | −25,941 | −1,448 | 23,046 |

**Reading:** under these assumptions, model-based targeting is far better than random or blanket
campaigns because it concentrates spend on likely churners. But the result **depends heavily on
the real retention success rate**: at 10% the selected threshold loses money, and a stricter
threshold (0.50) would be preferable. The operating threshold should be revisited once real costs,
margins and measured campaign effectiveness are known.

## 6. Limitations

- **Historical, static snapshot.** The dataset is a single IBM sample snapshot without dates; the
  "test set" contains historical customers whose outcome is already known. These are not live
  predictions on current customers.
- **Association, not causation.** SHAP explains model behaviour; it does not identify levers.
- **Missing context.** No data on customer service interactions, network quality, competitor offers,
  price changes or complaints, which are likely relevant drivers.
- **Moderate precision.** At the selected threshold about 44% of flagged customers would not have
  churned (precision 0.557), so offers should be inexpensive or targeted by driver.
- **Drift.** Patterns may change over time; the model would need monitoring and periodic retraining.
- **Fairness.** `SeniorCitizen` and `gender` are inputs; any real use should review whether
  treating customers differently based on them is appropriate and lawful.
